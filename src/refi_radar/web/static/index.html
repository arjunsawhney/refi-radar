How to read this page

Thousands of commercial property loans were taken out when interest rates were low. When a loan comes due, the owner has to refinance: take a new loan to pay off the old one. Today lenders will often lend less than what is owed, leaving a shortfall. Every shortfall is a deal Newmark can help with.

Three steps

  1. Pick a scenario: today's market, or a what-if such as offices emptying out.
  2. Read the headline numbers and charts: how much is due, how much is in trouble, when.
  3. Work the call list: click a row for the contact and what to say.

Glossary

Maturity
The date a loan must be repaid in full.
Refinance
Taking a new loan to repay the old one.
Existing loan (balloon)
What the owner still owes on the current loan when it comes due. It must be paid off, usually with a new loan.
Max new loan
The most a new lender would lend today. Lenders never lend 100% of value: it is the lowest of three tests (LTV, DSCR, debt yield), often 55-70% of the property's value.
Shortfall (refinance gap)
Existing loan minus max new loan: the cash the owner must find.
Hard to refinance
Needs New Capital + Distressed. Small Shortfall loans are not counted, because most owners can cover a gap of 10% or less themselves.
NOI
Net operating income: rent collected minus the costs of running the building.
Cap rate
The yield buyers expect: value = NOI ÷ cap rate. A higher cap rate means a lower value.
LTV
Loan-to-value: loan ÷ property value. Lenders typically cap it at 55-70%.
DSCR
Debt service coverage ratio: NOI ÷ annual loan payments. Lenders want about 1.25x or more; below 1.0x the building cannot pay its own loan.
Debt yield
NOI ÷ loan amount, a lender's quick safety check.
Recapitalization (recap)
Bringing in a new investor (joint venture or preferred equity) to fund the shortfall.
Preferred equity
Investor money that sits between the loan and the owner's equity, paid back before the owner.
Loan / note sale
A lender sells a troubled loan to another investor, often at a discount.
Agency loan
Apartment loans backed by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac.
bps
Basis points: 100 bps = 1 percentage point.
Window
The next 36 months from the as-of date. Loans due later are not shown.
